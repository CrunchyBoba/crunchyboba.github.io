About Floc Modeling

The Floc Modeling subteam aims to further our understanding of the mechanics of floc formation and clarification through the development of mathematical models of these processes. We focus on:

  • Distribution Analysis: Evaluating floc distribution within the clarifier using dye tracers
  • Model Development: Creating and refining clarification models to better describe primary particle removal
  • Experimental Validation: Testing theoretical models against real AguaClara plant data
  • Saturation Assessment: Determining whether floc saturation is uniformly distributed throughout the floc filter

Key Focus Areas

Flocculation Dynamics

Understanding how primary particles aggregate to form flocs and how coagulant affects this process

Clarification Modeling

Developing mathematical models that accurately predict particle removal efficiency in clarifiers

Floc Sweeping

Optimizing the floc sweeping mechanism where suspended flocs capture additional particles

Our Work

Spring 2026: Current Work

This semester, we are focusing on evaluating the distribution of flocs within the clarifier and refining our clarification models. Our key initiatives include:

Ongoing

Dye Tracer Experiments

Using methylene blue dye tracers to visualize and track floc formation and movement within the mini clarifier. This allows us to observe whether floc saturation varies with height and position in the clarifier.

Ongoing

Discretized Clarification Model Development

Developing a new discretized model that divides the floc filter into discrete boxes, each containing a single floc. This approach provides improved spatial resolution compared to continuous models.

Completed

Clarifier Design Improvements

Revised the mini clarifier design to address leakage issues and improve experimental reliability. Changes include: added lip for better acrylic seal, downward-sloping floc hopper, and removal of side sampling ports.

Completed

Model Fitting & Validation

Fitted both the Coupled Flocculation-Sedimentation model and the new Discretized Clarification model to plant operation data from AguaClara facilities in Nicaragua.

Key Results

  • Coupled Model RMSE: 0.1867 NTU (better at effluent prediction)
  • Discretized Model RMSE: 0.2299 NTU (better at coagulant dosage prediction)
  • Challenge: Both models predict extremely high coagulant coverage at low turbidities, suggesting missing mechanisms
Fall 2025

Dye Tracer Research & Clarifier Redesign

Evaluated Red-40 and methylene blue dyes for floc visualization. Determined that methylene blue (cationic) works best as it adsorbs to kaolin clay before flocculation. Identified design issues in mini clarifier and began implementing improvements.

Spring 2025

Continuous Clarification Model Refinement

Revised the existing continuous clarification model by introducing the nc parameter to account for multiple internal collisions between primary particles and flocs. Enhanced coagulant coverage calculations using the k' conversion constant.

Summer 2024 - Spring 2025

Foundation & Initial Development

Established baseline clarification models and experimental systems. Developed the low hydraulic residence time mini clarifier design that serves as the foundation for current experiments.

Upcoming Priorities

Next Semester

Complete Dye Tracer Experiments

Execute full experimental runs with methylene blue-dyed flocs to qualitatively and quantitatively assess floc distribution within the clarifier. Use image analysis to track spatial variations.

Next Semester

Resolve Coagulant Coverage Issue

Investigate why both models predict unrealistically high coagulant coverage at lower turbidities. Possible explanations include coagulant particles failing to encounter primary particles or remaining in suspension. This may require model modifications.

Future

Model Validation with Effluent Data

Obtain more comprehensive plant operation data that includes effluent turbidity measurements. This will allow more robust model fitting and reduce assumptions about target clarification.

Future

Advanced Optimization Methods

Explore global optimization methods and parameter consolidation strategies to improve model fitting efficiency and ensure we find truly optimal parameter sets.

Future

Plate Settler Integration

Incorporate lamellar sedimentation (plate settlers) more thoroughly into models, recognizing their importance in achieving clarified effluent below 1 NTU.

Reference Materials

Discretized Floc Filter Model

Primary particle concentration after clarification using discrete box approach:

C_p,clarified = C_p,flocculated × [1 - απ(3φ_floc/4π)^(2/3)]^(h_floc_filter/Λ)

Spring 2026 discretized model accounting for spatial variations

Spring 2025 Continuous Model

Exponential formulation for particle removal:

C_clarified = C_flocculated × exp[-k_c α_c h_floc_filter]

where k_c = πr²_floc × C_floc / m_floc

Attachment Efficiency

Three-component decomposition:

α = α_div × α_sat × α_coag α_sat = β(1 - P_sat)^(2/3) α_coag = 1 - (1 - Γ̃)²

Coagulant Coverage

Fractional coverage accounting for overlaps:

Γ̃ = 1 - (1 - r²_coag/(4r²_clay))^(R_clay × N_per_clay)

Floc Saturation

Saturation as steady-state balance:

P_sat = (C_P,flocculated - C_P) / (q(C_P,0 - C_P))

Coupled Flocculation-Sedimentation

Pennock et al. (2023) model:

C_P = (2π k₀ exp(κ(1-v_c/v_t,0)) α̅ G̃ θ × (ρ_P × π/6)^(-2/3) + C_P,0^(-2/3))^(-3/2)

Model Equivalence

The discretized and continuous Spring 2025 models are mathematically equivalent through first-order Taylor series approximation. Using Taylor series expansion on the discretized model:

Discretized ≈ e^0.027 × Continuous = 1.03 × Continuous

This means they produce essentially identical results but offer different computational advantages. The discretized approach excels at spatial analysis while the continuous form simplifies computation.

Efficiency & Hydraulic Parameters

v_c — Clarifier capture velocity (0.12 mm/s AguaClara standard)

Flocculation

Process where coagulant-destabilized primary particles collide and aggregate to form larger, settleable flocs. Energy dissipation in the flocculator creates velocity gradients that promote collisions.

Clarification

Process following flocculation where flocs are separated from treated water through settling and floc sweeping. AguaClara uses floc sweeping where suspended flocs capture additional particles.

Floc Sweeping

Mechanism using vertical water jets to maintain suspended flocs as a "floc filter." Primary particles travel upward through this suspension, colliding with flocs and being captured.

Floc Saturation

Measurement of how "full" a floc is - how many primary particles it has captured relative to its capacity. Saturated flocs are less effective at capturing additional particles.

Attachment Efficiency

Probability that a collision between a primary particle and a floc results in permanent attachment. Depends on coagulant coverage, floc saturation, and fluid dynamics.

Coagulation

Process where coagulant (typically PACl) destabilizes suspended colloidal particles by neutralizing their charge, allowing them to approach closely enough to aggregate into flocs.

Methylene Blue Tracing

Cationic dye that adsorbs strongly to negatively charged kaolin clay particles. By mixing dye with clay before coagulation, we create visibly distinguished tracer flocs for spatial analysis.

Plate Settlers

Lamellar sedimentation structure with parallel plates that captures smaller particles escaping the floc filter and recycles solids back, enabling effluent turbidity below 1 NTU.

Primary Particles

Individual clay, silt, or other particles in raw water that need to be removed. Typically much smaller than flocs (~ microns). Cannot settle quickly without flocculation.

Turbidity

Measure of water clarity - amount of suspended particles. 1 NTU ≈ 1.47 mg/L kaolin clay equivalent. Goal is to reduce raw water turbidity from hundreds of NTU to < 1 NTU.

Velocity Gradient (G)

Rate at which fluid velocity changes in space. Higher gradients promote more collisions between particles. Controlled in flocculator design through geometry and flow rate.

Hydraulic Residence Time (θ)

Average time a water particle spends in a reactor. Balance needed: long enough for flocs to form and settle, but short enough for practical plant operation.

Richardson & Zaki Theory

Our discretized floc filter model connections to classical sedimentation theory through particle separation distances. Richardson and Zaki (1954) derived drag force relationships for particles in suspension by modeling each particle within a hexagonal cell. This provides theoretical grounding for how floc concentration affects particle trajectories.

R = 1.126 × (b/C)^(1/3) where R is cell radius, b is particle radius, and C is concentration
